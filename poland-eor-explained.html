What an employer of record actually does in Poland — and what it does not

The short answer

An employer of record in Poland is a third-party entity that employs a worker on behalf of a client company. The EOR handles payroll, tax, social insurance and labour-law compliance while the client directs day-to-day work. That lets a foreign company hire in Poland without opening a local legal entity.

What does an employer of record do in Poland?

An EOR is normally used when a company wants to engage someone in Poland without employing that person through its own local entity. The EOR becomes the contractual employer and administers the employment relationship; the client company agrees the role, commercial terms and daily priorities with the worker.

A useful review starts with four separate questions. Who employs the person? Who directs and accepts the work? Who calculates and pays salary, tax and social insurance? Who owns the employment obligations? Keep those answers distinct. A polished administration flow does not replace the underlying legal documents.

Polish immigration rules add another boundary. Where a type A work permit is required, the official Polish business portal states that the employer intending to employ the foreign national submits the application. An intermediary label does not change that responsibility.

Are EOR employment and B2B services interchangeable in Poland?

Employment creates an employment relationship with employer payroll and labour-law obligations. B2B describes a supply of services between businesses. A Polish sole proprietorship, for example, is registered in CEIDG, is not a separate legal entity from its owner, and carries unlimited owner liability for business obligations, according to the official guide to business forms.

The distinction matters beyond the contract title. The official Polish guide to forms of business taxation discusses factors including liability for the result, direction over place and time of work, and economic risk when considering whether activity is genuinely business income. Calling a relationship "B2B" cannot, by itself, settle its legal or tax treatment.

Poly's proposed model coordinates B2B agreements, invoices and deductions. It should not be read as automatically creating ordinary employment. The final contractual route needs review against the actual work, the client relationship and the person's status.

Which documents should an EOR review include?

Request the documents that explain the arrangement in operational terms, not only a sales summary. The exact set depends on the route, but a consultation can begin with:

  • The employment or service agreement, including the parties and termination terms.
  • The client agreement and responsibility for scope, acceptance and day-to-day direction.
  • The payroll or invoice workflow, including currency, dates and supporting records.
  • A deduction breakdown that separates income tax, social insurance, health contributions and service fees.
  • Right-to-work documents where nationality, residence status or the role requires them.

These are review inputs, not a universal compliance checklist.

How should you compare EOR and B2B costs?

Compare the whole administration picture rather than one headline rate. An employment route can involve gross salary, employer costs and employment administration. A B2B route can involve contractor revenue, the provider fee, applicable PIT, ZUS, health contributions, VAT and the contractor's own business costs. Those items do not share one base, so subtracting a single percentage from an invoice is not a calculation.

Ask for a dated calculation tied to the proposed agreement and the person's confirmed profile. It should identify gross amounts, each deduction, any VAT treatment and the final recipient amount.

Questions to ask before choosing a Poland EOR route

A provider should be able to explain the model without hiding behind the term EOR. Ask which legal entity signs, whether the person becomes an employee or remains an independent service provider, who submits any required work-permit application, and who is responsible for correcting a calculation.

Also ask what happens when the role, working time, remuneration, client or residence status changes. The official work-permit procedure identifies circumstances in which a new permit can be required. Administration needs to respond to those changes rather than treating onboarding as a one-time form.

Finally, separate confirmed contractual commitments from proposed features.
